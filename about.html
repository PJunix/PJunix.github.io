About PJunix

A deliberate practice in understanding systems.

Security research is most useful when it leaves behind a clearer question, a tested observation, and a practical next step.

01

Who I am

PJunix is an independent cybersecurity learner documenting hands-on work across web applications, Linux systems, and security labs. This site is a working notebook: a place to turn experiments into concise, useful research notes.

It is intentionally grounded in learning. No assumed titles, credentials, or claims—just a growing body of practical investigation.

02

What I work on

The current work centers on how applications accept, process, and authorize input. That includes mapping attack surface, comparing requests and responses, and examining authentication and authorization behavior without losing sight of the underlying system.

Linux labs extend that process beyond the web boundary: enumeration, processes, services, permissions, and the conditions that make privilege escalation possible.

03

How I learn

I learn security by understanding behavior rather than memorizing payloads.

That means starting with a baseline, changing one variable at a time, recording observable differences, and validating a finding in a safe environment. Vulnerable machines and CTF-style labs offer room to make mistakes, retrace a path, and improve the documentation.

04

Current areas of focus

  • Manual web testing
  • Request/response analysis
  • SQL injection concepts
  • Authentication & authorization
  • Linux enumeration
  • Privilege escalation
  • Vulnerable lab design
  • Technical documentation

05

Security philosophy

Good security work is careful, scoped, and evidence-led. The aim is not simply to find a shortcut; it is to understand why a system behaves as it does and communicate that understanding clearly. Research here stays educational, controlled, and respectful of real-world boundaries.
